## How the four images stay consistent

Each prompt is built as `[scene description] + [shared art-direction block]`. The shared block is identical across all four, which is what makes the set read as one visual family:

- **Palette** — deep indigo-black ground (#05070F), electric cyan (#22E0FF), ultraviolet purple (#A66BFF), mint green (#5EF2C4), one amber highlight (#FFC24B) reserved for whatever the viewer should look at first.
- **Material** — frosted glassmorphism panels with 1px luminous edges, floating in space.
- **Light & depth** — volumetric cinematic lighting, soft bloom, shallow depth of field, a thin holographic grid receding behind the subject.
- **Camera** — 3/4 isometric, consistent across all four so the stages feel like one continuous rig.

Colour coding is load-bearing, not decorative: cyan is always Keys and the machine side, ultraviolet is always Queries and attention, mint is always Values and the human-readable side, amber always marks the single thing being highlighted (a hot attention cell, a mask, a winning prediction).

For DALL·E 3, drop the trailing `--ar 16:9 --style raw --v 6` and ask for a wide 16:9 image in the sentence instead.

## Stage 01 — Embeddings & Position

**From letters to located vectors**

A Transformer never sees your sentence. It sees a stack of numbers — and until we tell it otherwise, that stack has no word order at all.

### Analogy — the library card catalogue

Imagine every book in a library is described by a card carrying scores for its themes: how adventurous, how romantic, how technical. Two books with similar cards sit near each other on the shelf, even if their titles share no words. That card is the **embedding**.

But a catalogue card says nothing about where the book sits on the shelf. So the librarian stamps a shelf coordinate onto every card. That stamp is **positional encoding** — and it is why *“The man drove the woman to the store”* stops meaning the same thing as *“The woman drove the man to the store.”*

### Technical explanation

Text is split into **tokens**, each mapped to an integer ID, and each ID looks up a learned vector of length `d_model`. Similar meanings end up in similar directions in that space — the classic *king / queen / man / woman* geometry from the slides.

Because the encoder reads every token **in parallel**, it has no built-in notion of order. A sinusoidal positional signal is therefore **added** to each embedding: even dimensions get a sine, odd dimensions get a cosine, each at a different frequency. The sum of the two is what enters the first attention layer.

### Formula

```latex
\begin{aligned}
PE_{(pos,\,2i)} &= \sin\!\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{\text{model}}}}\right) \\[4pt]
PE_{(pos,\,2i+1)} &= \cos\!\left(\frac{pos}{10000^{2i/d_{\text{model}}}}\right) \\[6pt]
Z^{(0)} &= \text{Embedding}(x) + PE
\end{aligned}
```

## Stage 02 — Multi-Head Self-Attention

**Every word interrogates every other word**

Attention answers one question, for every token at once: which parts of this input matter to me right now?

### Analogy — the research student in the library

A student walks in with a question written on a slip of paper: that is the **Query**. Every shelf carries a label — Fruits, Electronics, Cleaning — and those labels are the **Keys**. The student compares the slip against every label at a glance and scores the match.

The books actually pulled off the matching shelves are the **Values**. The student doesn't take one book and ignore the rest; they take a weighted blend — 70% from the shelf that matched best, a little from the others. Multi-head attention is simply several students sent in at once, each briefed to look for something different: one tracks grammar, another tracks who-did-what-to-whom.

### Technical explanation

Each token projects its vector three ways to produce a Query, a Key and a Value. Scoring every query against every key gives an n×n grid of raw compatibilities. Dividing by `√d` keeps those scores in a range where softmax stays sensitive rather than saturating, and softmax turns each row into weights that sum to 1.

Those weights are then applied to the Values, so each output vector is a context-aware mixture of the whole sentence — which is how *“it”* ends up pointing at *“animal”*. Heads run in parallel, get concatenated, and pass through a linear layer; a residual connection plus layer normalisation (**Add & Norm**) keeps the signal stable.

### Formula

```latex
\begin{aligned}
\text{Attention}(Q,K,V) &= \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{T}}{\sqrt{d}}\right)V \\[6pt]
\text{head}_i &= \text{Attention}(XW_i^{Q},\, XW_i^{K},\, XW_i^{V}) \\[4pt]
\text{MultiHead}(X) &= \text{Concat}(\text{head}_1,\dots,\text{head}_h)\,W^{O}
\end{aligned}
```

## Stage 03 — Cross-Attention & Masking

**The decoder writes, one token at a time**

The decoder is allowed to look at the entire input sentence — but only at the part of its own output it has already written.

### Analogy — the simultaneous interpreter

An interpreter listens to a full sentence in English and speaks it back in French. While speaking, she constantly glances back at what the speaker said — that glance is **cross-attention**: her Queries come from the French she is producing, while the Keys and Values come from the English she heard.

What she cannot do is quote the French words she hasn't said yet. Training a decoder without that restriction would be like an exam where the answer sheet is face-up on the desk. The **look-ahead mask** is the sheet of paper laid over the future words.

### Technical explanation

The decoder block runs two attention layers. The first is **masked** self-attention over the target sequence: before softmax, every position above the diagonal is set to −∞, so those cells come out as exactly 0 probability. That is the triangular pattern in the slides.

The second is the **cross-attention** layer. Its Queries come from the decoder, while its Keys and Values come from the encoder's output — the sequence of context-rich embeddings the encoder produced. This is the bridge between understanding the input and generating the output.

### Formula

```latex
\begin{aligned}
M_{ij} &= \begin{cases} 0 & j \le i \\ -\infty & j > i \end{cases} \\[6pt]
\text{Masked}(Q,K,V) &= \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{QK^{T}}{\sqrt{d}} + M\right)V \\[6pt]
\text{Cross}(Q_{dec},K_{enc},V_{enc}) &= \text{softmax}\!\left(\frac{Q_{dec}K_{enc}^{T}}{\sqrt{d}}\right)V_{enc}
\end{aligned}
```

## Stage 04 — Softmax & Prediction

**One vector becomes one word**

Whatever else it is doing, the model's final act is always the same: turn a vector into a probability for every word it knows, and pick one.

### Analogy — the talent-show scoreboard

Every word in the vocabulary walks on stage and receives a raw score from the judges. The scores are unbounded and hard to read — one contestant might get 8.4, another −3.1. The **linear layer** is the panel of judges; those raw scores are the **logits**.

**Softmax** is the scoreboard that converts scores into percentages summing to 100%. **Temperature** is the judges' mood: cold and strict, and the favourite takes almost everything; warm and generous, and the outsiders get a real chance. Then the winner is appended to the sentence and the entire show runs again for the next word — until `` wins.

### Technical explanation

The decoder's final hidden vector is multiplied by a linear classifier whose output width equals the vocabulary size — tens of thousands of logits, one per token. Softmax normalises them into a probability distribution.

Greedy decoding takes the argmax; sampling draws from the distribution, which is why the same prompt can produce different text. Because the chosen token is fed back in as the next input, generation is **autoregressive**: this loop, repeated at scale over a transformer-based model trained on massive text with billions of parameters, is what an **LLM** is.

### Formula

```latex
\begin{aligned}
\text{logits} &= h_{t}W^{T} + b \in \mathbb{R}^{|V|} \\[6pt]
P(w_i \mid x) &= \frac{e^{\text{logit}_i / T}}{\sum_{j=1}^{|V|} e^{\text{logit}_j / T}} \\[6pt]
\hat{w}_t &= \arg\max_i P(w_i \mid x)
\end{aligned}
```

### What the app computes

Every number on screen is derived from the sentence typed in the sidebar — tokens, UTF-8 bits, embeddings, sinusoidal positions, Q/K/V projections, scaled dot-product scores, softmax weights, masked and cross attention, and the final probability distribution. Weight matrices are pseudo-random but seeded by the token string and the seed control, so the same sentence always produces the same figures — repeatable in front of a class.

Attention patterns are therefore *structurally* real and *semantically* arbitrary: the mechanism is exact, but the weights are untrained. Worth saying out loud to students, so nobody reads meaning into which word attends to which.
